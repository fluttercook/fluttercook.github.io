This page explains what data is collected when you visit trunghieu-it.blogspot.com, who receives it, and what you can do about it.

The blog is written and run by Trung Hieu, a mobile developer working with Flutter, iOS and Android. It is a personal blog, not a company. Contact: trunghieu4121993@gmail.com.

I run no servers of my own and hold no user database. The whole blog lives on Google's Blogger platform. Everything described below is collected and controlled by Google or by Google's advertising partners, not by me.

Who collects what

1. Platform and hosting — Blogger (Google)

The blog runs on Blogger, a Google service. Like any hosting provider, Google receives your IP address, your browser's user-agent string and the page you asked for, and logs them to serve the site and to protect it from abuse. Blogger also has built-in pageview stats, which I see only in aggregate — views per post, per country, per referrer — never a list of readers.

See the Google Privacy Policy.

2. Advertising — Google AdSense

The blog shows ads served by Google AdSense. That means:

  • Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this blog or other websites.
  • Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visit to this blog and/or other sites on the Internet.
  • You may opt out of personalised advertising at Google Ads Settings.
  • You may opt out of third-party vendors' use of cookies for personalised advertising at aboutads.info/choices or optout.networkadvertising.org.

How Google handles the data it collects is described in How Google uses information from sites that use its services.

3. Comments

Comments use Blogger's built-in comment system. When you comment, Google receives and stores your display name (or your Google account), the comment text, a timestamp and your IP address. That data sits in my Blogger account and on Google's infrastructure; I can moderate or delete comments but cannot export that data anywhere. Do not put sensitive personal information in a comment. Email me if you want a comment of yours removed.

4. Third-party resources the page loads

Rendering a page also makes your browser call a few other servers. Those calls by themselves reveal your IP address and user-agent to whoever runs them:

  • fonts.gstatic.com — the theme's typefaces (Google Fonts).
  • resources.blogblog.com, www.blogger.com, blogger.googleusercontent.com — Blogger's own CSS, JavaScript and images.
  • pagead2.googlesyndication.com — the AdSense script.
  • www.gstatic.com, apis.google.com — Google helper libraries the theme uses (the copy-code button, share buttons).

All of them are Google services.

5. What this blog does NOT have

Stated plainly, because this is the part other sites tend to overstate: this blog does not run Google Analytics, does not run a Facebook pixel, has no third-party tracking pixel other than AdSense, has no email newsletter, has no signup form, and does not sell or share data with anyone for money.

If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland

Where the law requires it, a consent message appears before any personalised advertising cookie is set, in line with Google's EU user consent policy. That message always carries a reject button as prominent as the accept button. You can change your choice at any time by clearing this site's cookies in your browser, which brings the message back.

Legal bases: your consent for advertising cookies, and legitimate interest for the technical logs Google keeps to serve and protect the site. You have the right to access, correct, delete or object to the processing of your personal data, and the right to lodge a complaint with the supervisory authority where you live.

Because I hold no user database, requests about advertising or platform data should go directly to Google, which controls that data. Comments on the blog are the exception — email me and I can delete them.

If you are in California

I do not sell personal information for money. The use of advertising cookies may count as "sharing" for cross-context behavioural advertising under the CCPA/CPRA. You can opt out through the Google Ads Settings link above, or by sending a Global Privacy Control signal from your browser — that signal is honoured wherever it is passed through.

Children

This blog is written for developers and students and is not directed at children under 13. I do not knowingly collect data from children.

Retention and international transfers

I hold no personal data myself, so there is nothing for me to retain or delete on your behalf. Google processes data on its own retention schedules and may transfer it outside your country, including to the United States, under the safeguards it publishes.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the date at the top of the page changes with it.
